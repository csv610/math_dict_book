\chapter*{O}
\label{chap:o}

\term{Object}
An entity in a category that is acted on by morphisms; in set theory, any set is an object.

\term{Occurrence}
An instance of a symbol or term at a particular position in a formula or expression.

\term{Octonion}
An 8-dimensional non-associative division algebra over the real numbers. Octonions extend quaternions and satisfy the property that every non-zero element has a multiplicative inverse. They are used in high-dimensional geometry and string theory.

\term{Octahedron}
A Platonic solid with 8 equilateral triangular faces, 6 vertices, and 12 edges. It is the dual of the cube. Its symmetry group is the octahedral group of order 48.

\term{Octave}
In group theory, an informal name for an octonion; more generally a doubling of dimension in normed algebra structure.

\term{Odd Permutation}
A permutation that can be expressed as the product of an odd number of transpositions. The set of even permutations forms the alternating group $A_n$. The sign of an odd permutation is $-1$.

\term{One-Point Compactification}
The process of embedding a locally compact Hausdorff space $X$ into a compact space $X^*$ by adding a single "point at infinity" $\infty$. A neighborhood of $\infty$ is the complement of a compact set in $X$.

\term{One-Sided Limit}
The limit of a function as the argument approaches a value from either the right ($\lim_{x \to a^+}$) or the left ($\lim_{x \to a^-}$). A function is continuous at $a$ iff both one-sided limits exist and equal $f(a)$.

\term{Operad}
An algebraic structure that models operations with multiple inputs and one output. Operads are used in homotopy theory, quantum field theory, and the study of $A_\infty$-algebras.

\term{Open Cover}
A collection of open sets whose union contains the entire space $X$. A space is compact if every open cover has a finite subcover.

\term{Open Interval}
An interval $(a, b)$ that excludes its endpoints.

\term{Open Mapping Theorem}
A fundamental result in functional analysis: every surjective bounded linear operator between Banach spaces is an open map (it maps open sets to open sets).

\term{Open Problem}
A mathematical question that has not yet been resolved, such as the Riemann hypothesis.

\term{Open Set}
A subset $U$ of a topological space such that for every $x \in U$, there exists a neighborhood of $x$ contained in $U$. In a metric space, a set is open if every point is the center of some open ball contained in the set.

\term{Operation}
A rule that combines elements of a set to produce a new element, such as addition or composition.

\term{Operator (Linear)}
A linear map $T: V \to W$ between two vector spaces. In the context of Hilbert spaces, the term often refers to a bounded operator $T: H \to H$.

\term{Operator Algebra}
The theory of algebras of bounded operators on Hilbert space, including C*-algebras and von Neumann algebras.

\term{Operator Norm}
The norm of a linear operator $T$ defined as $\|T\| = \sup_{\|x\|=1} \|Tx\|$. It represents the maximum "stretch" factor of the operator.

\term{Orbit (Group Action)}
The set of all points in $X$ to which a point $x$ can be moved by the action of a group $G$: $\text{Orb}(x) = \{g \cdot x : g \in G\}$. The orbit-stabilizer theorem relates the size of the orbit to the size of the stabilizer.

\term{Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE)}
A differential equation containing one or more functions of one independent variable and their derivatives. This contrasts with partial differential equations (PDEs), which involve functions of multiple variables.

\term{Orthogonal Complement}
For a subspace $V$ of an inner product space $W$, the orthogonal complement $V^\perp$ is the set of all vectors $w \in W$ such that $\langle w, v \rangle = 0$ for all $v \in V$. $W$ is the direct sum $V \oplus V^\perp$.

\term{Orthogonal Group}
The group $O(n)$ of $n \times n$ orthogonal matrices. It consists of all linear transformations of $\mathbb{R}^n$ that preserve the Euclidean inner product and distance.

\term{Orthogonal Matrix}
A square matrix $Q$ such that $Q^T Q = Q Q^T = I$. The columns of an orthogonal matrix form an orthonormal basis for $\mathbb{R}^n$, and its determinant is either $1$ or $-1$.

\term{Orthogonal Projection}
A linear operator $P$ such that $P^2 = P$ and $\langle Px, y \rangle = \langle x, Py \rangle$. It projects a vector onto a subspace along the orthogonal complement.

\term{Orthogonality}
The property of two vectors $u, v$ having an inner product of zero: $\langle u, v \rangle = 0$. Geometrically, this means the vectors are perpendicular.

\term{Orthonormal Basis}
A basis for an inner product space where every pair of distinct vectors is orthogonal and each vector has unit norm. Every finite-dimensional inner product space possesses an orthonormal basis (via Gram--Schmidt).

\term{Osculating Circle}
The circle that best approximates a curve $\gamma(t)$ at a given point $p$. Its radius is the reciprocal of the curvature $\kappa$ at $p$, and its center is the center of curvature.

\term{Outer Measure}
A function $\mu^*$ that assigns a value to all subsets of a set, satisfying monotonicity and countable subadditivity. Carathéodory's criterion uses outer measures to define a $\sigma$-algebra of measurable sets.

\term{Overdetermined System}
A system of linear equations $Ax = b$ that has more equations than unknowns. Such systems typically have no solution unless the vector $b$ lies in the column space of $A$.

\term{Oval}
A plane curve that is closed, convex, and smooth. All ellipses are ovals, but not all ovals are ellipses.

\term{O-minimal Structure}
A structure in model theory where every definable set in one dimension is a finite union of points and open intervals. This prevents "wild" behavior like the sine function's infinite oscillations.

\term{Order (Group Theory)}
The number of elements in a group $G$, denoted $|G|$. For an element $g \in G$, the order is the smallest positive integer $n$ such that $g^n = e$.

\term{Order (Field)}
A binary relation $\leq$ on a field that is a total order and is compatible with addition and multiplication ($a \leq b \implies a+c \leq b+c$ and $a, b \geq 0 \implies ab \geq 0$).

\term{Order of Growth}
The asymptotic behavior of a function as its argument tends to infinity, typically expressed using Big O notation $O(f(n))$. It is used to compare the efficiency of algorithms.

\term{Order-Preserving Map}
A function $f$ between partially ordered sets such that $x \leq y \implies f(x) \leq f(y)$. Also known as a monotone map.

\term{Order-Statistic}
In statistics, the $k$-th smallest value in a sample of $n$ independent and identically distributed random variables. The sample median is the $n/2$-th order statistic.

\term{Ordered Pair}
The pair $(a, b)$ where order matters: $(a,b) = (c,d)$ iff $a=c$ and $b=d$.

\term{Ordering}
A reflexive, antisymmetric, transitive relation, called a partial order; a total order also compares every pair.

\term{Ordinary Least Squares (OLS)}
The method of estimating the coefficients of a linear regression model by minimizing the sum of the squares of the vertical deviations between the observed responses and the fitted line.

\term{Orthocenter}
The point where the three altitudes of a triangle intersect. For an obtuse triangle, the orthocenter lies outside the triangle.

\term{Orthogonal Polynomials}
A sequence of polynomials $\{p_n\}$ that are orthogonal with respect to some weight function $w(x)$ on an interval: $\int w(x) p_n(x) p_m(x) \, dx = 0$ for $n \neq m$. Examples include Legendre and Chebyshev polynomials.

\term{Orlicz Space}
A generalization of $L^p$ spaces defined using a Young function $\Phi$ instead of a power $x^p$. They are used in the study of PDEs and harmonic analysis.

\term{Orthonormal Set}
A set of vectors where every pair of distinct vectors is orthogonal and each vector is a unit vector. Any orthonormal set is linearly independent.

\term{Orthonormality}
The property of a set of vectors that are pairwise orthogonal and each of unit norm, forming an orthonormal basis.

\term{Outlier}
An observation in a dataset that lies an abnormal distance from other values. Outliers can be detected using the interquartile range or Z-scores.

\term{Outer Product}
The operation that takes two vectors $u, v$ and produces a matrix $M = uv^T$ where $M_{ij} = u_i v_j$. It is the dual to the inner product.

\term{Outer Automorphism}
An automorphism of a group $G$ that is not an inner automorphism. The group of outer automorphisms is $\text{Out}(G) = \text{Aut}(G)/\text{Inn}(G)$.

\term{Overlap}
A region common to two or more sets or intervals, in measure theory the degree to which measurable sets intersect.
