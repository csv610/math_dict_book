\chapter*{J}
\label{chap:j}

\term{Jacobi Identity}
For a Lie algebra, $[X,[Y,Z]] + [Y,[Z,X]] + [Z,[X,Y]] = 0$.

\term{Jacobi Symbol}
A generalization of the Legendre symbol to composite denominators, written $(\frac{a}{n})$.

\term{Jacobian Matrix}
The matrix containing all first-order partial derivatives of a vector-valued function $f: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^m$. It provides the best linear approximation to the function at a given point and is central to the Inverse Function Theorem.

\term{Jacobian Determinant}
The determinant of the Jacobian matrix for a map $f: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^n$. It represents the local volume scaling factor of the transformation and is used in the change of variables formula for multiple integrals.

\term{Jacobian Variety}
An abelian variety attached to a compact Riemann surface, parametrizing line bundles of degree zero.

\term{Jacobson Radical}
The intersection of all maximal left ideals of a ring $R$, or equivalently, the intersection of all maximal right ideals. It consists of elements that annihilate all simple left $R$-modules. A ring is semisimple iff its Jacobson radical is zero.

\term{Jaccard Index}
A measure of similarity between two sets $A$ and $B$, defined as the size of the intersection divided by the size of the union: $J(A,B) = |A \cap B| / |A \cup B|$. Used extensively in ecology, data science, and clustering.

\term{Jensen's Formula}
The identity $\int_0^1 \log|f(e^{2\pi i t})|\, dt = \log|f(0)| + \sum \log(1/|a_k|)$ relating a holomorphic function to its zeros in the unit disk.

\term{Jensen's Inequality}
For a convex function $f$, the value of the function at the average is less than or equal to the average of the function values: $f(\mathbb{E}[X]) \leq \mathbb{E}[f(X)]$. This is a fundamental result in probability and optimization.

\term{Jet}
In differential geometry, a jet is an equivalence class of functions that agree up to a certain order of differentiation at a point. An $k$-jet at $p$ captures the Taylor polynomial of degree $k$ of the function at $p$.

\term{Jet Bundle}
A bundle whose fibers are the jets of sections of another bundle. It provides a geometric framework for studying partial differential equations, as a PDE can be viewed as a sub-bundle of a jet bundle.

\term{Jet Space}
The space of all possible jets of a given order. It generalizes the notion of tangent space by including information about higher-order derivatives.

\term{J-invariant}
A modular invariant of elliptic curves, $j(\tau) = 1728 \frac{g_2^3}{g_2^3 - 27g_3^2}$, which classifies elliptic curves over $\mathbb{C}$ up to isomorphism. Two curves are isomorphic iff they have the same $j$-invariant.

\term{Joint Distribution}
A probability distribution that describes the simultaneous behavior of two or more random variables. It is specified by a joint probability mass function (discrete) or a joint probability density function (continuous).

\term{Joint Entropy}
The entropy $H(X,Y)$ of a pair of random variables, measuring the combined uncertainty.

\term{Joint Probability Distribution}
The probability distribution of a set of random variables. For variables $X$ and $Y$, it is $P(X \leq x, Y \leq y)$. Marginal distributions are obtained by integrating (or summing) over the other variables.

\term{Joint Spectral Radius}
The maximum asymptotic growth rate of products of matrices from a finite set $\mathcal{M}$. It is defined as $\rho(\mathcal{M}) = \lim_{k \to \infty} \max_{A_i \in \mathcal{M}} \|A_k \cdots A_1\|^{1/k}$. Used in stability analysis of switched linear systems.

\term{Jordan Algebra}
A non-associative algebra where the product satisfies $xy = yx$ and the Jordan identity $(xy)x^2 = x(yx^2)$. Originally motivated by the observables in quantum mechanics.

\term{Jordan Block}
A square matrix in Jordan normal form with a single eigenvalue $\lambda$ on the diagonal and $1$s on the super-diagonal. It represents the simplest possible structure for a linear operator that cannot be diagonalized.

\term{Jordan Canonical Form}
The block-diagonal normal form of a matrix over an algebraically closed field, consisting of Jordan blocks for each eigenvalue.

\term{Jordan Normal Form}
A block-diagonal matrix where each block is a Jordan block. Every square matrix over an algebraically closed field is similar to a matrix in Jordan normal form, unique up to the ordering of the blocks.

\term{Jordan Curve Theorem}
A fundamental result in topology stating that every simple closed curve in the plane divides the plane into exactly two regions: an "inside" (bounded) and an "outside" (unbounded).

\term{Jordan Decomposition}
The decomposition of a linear operator into a diagonalizable part and a nilpotent part.

\term{Jordan Domain}
A bounded region in the complex plane whose boundary is a Jordan curve. By the Riemann Mapping Theorem, any Jordan domain is conformally equivalent to the unit disk.

\term{Jordan Measure}
A generalization of the concept of area/volume to sets in $\mathbb{R}^n$, defined via the difference between inner and outer approximations by finite unions of rectangles. A set is Jordan measurable iff its boundary has measure zero.

\term{Jordan--Brouwer Separation Theorem}
A generalization of the Jordan Curve Theorem to higher dimensions: a topological sphere $S^{n-1}$ embedded in $\mathbb{R}^n$ divides the space into two connected components.

\term{Jordan--Hölder Theorem}
A theorem in group theory stating that any two composition series of a finite group are equivalent: they have the same length and isomorphic composition factors.

\term{Jordan--von Neumann Theorem}
A characterization of Hilbert spaces: a normed space is a Hilbert space iff its norm satisfies the parallelogram law: $2(\|x\|^2 + \|y\|^2) = \|x+y\|^2 + \|x-y\|^2$.

\term{J-homomorphism}
A map from the homotopy groups of a Lie group to the stable homotopy groups of spheres. It is used in algebraic topology to study the stable stems of the sphere spectrum.

\term{Join (Lattice Theory)}
The least upper bound of two elements in a lattice, denoted $a \vee b$. It is the smallest element $x$ such that $a \leq x$ and $b \leq x$.

\term{Join (Geometry)}
The operation of creating a line, plane, or subspace that contains two given subspaces. For two points $P, Q$, the join is the unique line passing through both.

\term{Join Semilattice}
A partially ordered set in which every pair of elements has a least upper bound.

\term{Joukowski Transform}
The conformal map $w = z + 1/z$, used in aerodynamics.

\term{Julia Set}
In complex dynamics, the set of points where the iterates of a holomorphic function $f$ do not form a normal family. It often exhibits fractal structure and is the boundary of the Fatou set.

\term{Jump Discontinuity}
A type of discontinuity where the left-hand and right-hand limits of a function exist but are not equal. The "jump" is the difference between these two limits.

\term{Jump Function}
A step function whose value changes discontinuously at a set of points.

\term{Jump Process}
A stochastic process that evolves by taking discrete jumps at random times, rather than moving continuously. Examples include the Poisson process and Markov jump processes.

\term{Junction}
A point of a graph where three or more edges meet.

\term{J-spectrum}
In operator theory, the part of the spectrum of a $J$-self-adjoint operator that is invariant under the action of the fundamental symmetry $J$.
