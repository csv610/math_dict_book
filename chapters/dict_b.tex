\chapter*{B}
\label{chap:b}

\term{Baire Category Theorem}
A topological space is a Baire space if every countable intersection of open dense sets is dense. Complete metric spaces and locally compact Hausdorff spaces are Baire spaces, so a countable union of nowhere dense sets has empty interior.

\term{Banach Space}
A complete normed vector space, meaning every Cauchy sequence converges. Hilbert spaces are the Banach spaces whose norm comes from an inner product.

\term{Banach--Steinhaus Theorem}
Also called the uniform boundedness principle, it states that for a family of bounded linear operators on a Banach space, pointwise boundedness implies uniform boundedness on the unit ball. It is a consequence of Baire's theorem.

\term{Banach--Tarski Paradox}
A unit ball in $\mathbb{R}^3$ can be decomposed into finitely many pieces and reassembled, using only rigid motions, into two disjoint copies of the original ball. The pieces are necessarily non-measurable, and the construction uses the axiom of choice.

\term{Base Case}
In induction, the initial case that is verified directly. Once the base case holds and the induction step is proved, the statement holds for all natural numbers.

\term{Basis}
A linearly independent spanning set of a vector space. Every vector space has a basis, and any two bases have the same cardinality, called the dimension.

\term{Bayes' Theorem}
The formula $P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(B \mid A) P(A)}{P(B)}$ relating conditional probabilities. It is the foundation of Bayesian statistics and inference.

\term{Bernoulli Numbers}
The rational numbers $B_n$ defined by $\frac{x}{e^x - 1} = \sum_{n \geq 0} B_n \frac{x^n}{n!}$. They appear in the Euler--Maclaurin formula and in the values of the zeta function at negative integers.

\term{Bernoulli Trial}
An experiment with exactly two outcomes, conventionally called success and failure, with probabilities $p$ and $1-p$. Repeating independent Bernoulli trials gives a Bernoulli process.

\term{Berry's Paradox}
The phrase "the smallest positive integer not definable in fewer than twenty words" seems to define a number in fewer than twenty words, a contradiction. It illustrates the tension between definition and countability and relates to definability arguments in logic.

\term{Bertrand's Paradox}
Different natural procedures for choosing a "random chord" of a circle (by endpoint, by midpoint, by distance from center) yield different probabilities for a chord being longer than a side of the inscribed equilateral triangle. It highlights the ambiguity of uniform randomness without a precise specification.

\term{Besov Space}
A scale of function spaces $B^s_{p,q}$, with smoothness $s \in \mathbb{R}$ and $1 \le p, q \le \infty$, whose norms measure smoothness through the $\ell^q$ norm over dyadic scales of the $\ell^p$ norms of the Littlewood--Paley or wavelet blocks of $f$. Sobolev spaces sit inside the scale, with $W^{k,p} = B^k_{p,p}$ for integer $k$ in many settings, and Besov spaces interpolate between them. They are the natural spaces for the regularity theory of PDEs, for Fourier restriction estimates, and for nonlinear approximation in harmonic analysis.

\term{Bessel Function}
The solutions $J_n$ and $Y_n$ of Bessel's differential equation $x^2 y'' + x y' + (x^2 - n^2) y = 0$. They are used to solve boundary value problems in cylindrical coordinates.

\term{Bijection}
A function that is both injective and surjective, pairing each element of its domain with exactly one element of its codomain and vice versa.

\term{Bilinear Form}
A map $B: V \times W \to k$ that is linear in each argument separately. When $V = W$ and $B(v,w) = B(w,v)$, the form is symmetric.

\term{Binary Operation}
A function $f: S \times S \to S$ combining two elements of a set to produce a third. Group, ring, and lattice operations are binary operations.

\term{Binary Relation}
A set of ordered pairs $(a, b)$ with $a \in A$, $b \in B$, expressing a relationship between elements of two sets. Functions are a special class of binary relations.

\term{Binomial Coefficient}
The number $\binom{n}{k} = \frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}$ of ways to choose $k$ elements from a set of $n$, also the coefficient of $x^k$ in $(1+x)^n$.

\term{Binomial Distribution}
The distribution of the number of successes in $n$ independent Bernoulli trials, with probability mass function $P(X = k) = \binom{n}{k} p^k (1-p)^{n-k}$.

\term{Binomial Theorem}
The expansion $(x + y)^n = \sum_{k=0}^n \binom{n}{k} x^{n-k} y^k$. It generalises to arbitrary real exponents via the binomial series.

\term{Bipartite Graph}
A graph whose vertices can be partitioned into two sets such that every edge joins a vertex in one set to a vertex in the other. Bipartite graphs have no odd cycles.

\term{Birch--Swinnerton-Dyer Conjecture}
The conjecture asserting that for an elliptic curve $E$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ the order of vanishing of its Hasse--Weil $L$-function $L(E,s)$ at $s=1$, the analytic rank, equals the rank of the finitely generated abelian group $E(\mathbb{Q})$, the Mordell--Weil group. It further predicts the leading coefficient of $L(E,s)$ at $s=1$ in terms of the regulator, the Tate--Shafarevich group, and the Tamagawa numbers. Verified in many rank-zero and rank-one cases, it is one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems and remains open in general.

\term{Birthday Paradox}
With only 23 people in a room the probability that two share a birthday exceeds $1/2$, and with 70 it exceeds $0.999$, far smaller than intuition suggests. The exact value is $1 - \prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(365-k)/365$.

\term{Borel Measure}
A measure defined on the Borel $\sigma$-algebra of a topological space. On Polish spaces and on locally compact Hausdorff spaces, finite Borel measures are inner regular on open sets and outer regular on Borel sets, and regular measures coincide with tight ones. Lebesgue measure restricted to the Borel sets of $\mathbb{R}^n$ is the fundamental example. By the Riesz representation theorem, positive linear functionals on compactly supported continuous functions correspond bijectively to regular Borel measures.

\term{Borel Set}
A set that can be obtained from open sets by countably many operations of union, intersection, and complement. Borel sets are measurable for every measure on the reals.

\term{Borel--Cantelli Lemmas}
Two lemmas about events $E_n$ in a probability space. If $\sum_n P(E_n) < \infty$, then infinitely many of the events occur with probability $0$; conversely, if the events are independent and $\sum_n P(E_n) = \infty$, then infinitely many occur with probability $1$.

\term{Borsuk--Ulam Theorem}
The theorem that every continuous map from the $n$-sphere $S^n$ to $\mathbb{R}^n$ identifies a pair of antipodal points, so the $n$-sphere cannot be embedded in $\mathbb{R}^n$. In two dimensions it says some pair of antipodal points on a sphere have the same value under any continuous real-valued map on the surface, which implies the ham sandwich theorem.

\term{Bound}
An upper or lower constraint on the values of a function, sequence, or set. A set is bounded above if it has an upper bound, bounded if it has both upper and lower bounds.

\term{Boundary}
For a set $S$ in a topological space, the set of points whose every neighbourhood meets both $S$ and its complement. The boundary of a manifold is its edge.

\term{Boundary-value Problem}
A differential equation together with conditions imposed on the solution at the boundary of its domain, such as Dirichlet or Neumann conditions.

\term{Bounded Function}
A function whose image is contained in a bounded interval, so there exist $m, M$ with $m \leq f(x) \leq M$ for all $x$ in its domain.

\term{Bounded Linear Operator}
A linear map $T : X \to Y$ between normed spaces that is continuous, equivalently bounded: there is $C$ with $\|Tx\|_Y \le C\|x\|_X$ for all $x$, so $T$ is bounded on the unit ball and $\|T\| = \sup_{\|x\| \le 1}\|Tx\|$ defines the operator norm. The space of such operators between Banach spaces is a Banach space. The Banach--Steinhaus theorem, the open mapping theorem, and the bounded inverse theorem are fundamental consequences of these hypotheses, and the continuous adjoint $T^*$ exists whenever the spaces are normed.

\term{Boy or Girl Paradox}
The answer to the probability question about two children changes depending on how the information "one is a girl" is obtained: if a random child is a girl the probability of two girls is $1/2$, but if "at least one is a girl" is asserted it is $1/3$. The difference is a subtlety of conditional probability.

\term{Braess's Paradox}
Adding a new road (or capacity) to a congested network can increase every traveller's travel time, because selfish routing equilibria need not improve with more options. It is a classic counterintuitive result in network flow and game theory.

\term{Branch Point}
A point of the domain of a multi-valued analytic function at which the function cannot be defined continuously in any neighbourhood without values repeating, as at $z = 0$ for $\sqrt{z}$ or $\log z$.

\term{Brouwer Fixed-Point Theorem}
The theorem that every continuous map from a closed ball in $\mathbb{R}^n$ to itself has a fixed point. It is a landmark result in topology with applications in economics and PDE theory.

\term{Brownian Motion}
The continuous-time stochastic process with independent stationary Gaussian increments, the mathematical model of random diffusion.

\term{Burali-Forti Paradox}
The set of all ordinal numbers would itself be an ordinal, strictly larger than every member including itself, a contradiction. It shows the ordinals form a proper class, and motivated the ZF axioms with rank-based set formation.

\term{Burnside's Lemma}
The formula $|X/G| = \frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g \in G} |X^g|$ for the number of orbits of a group action, where $X^g$ is the fixed set of $g$. It is used to count distinct objects under symmetry.

\term{Busy Beaver}
The maximum number of steps (or 1s written) a halting Turing machine with $n$ states can produce. The function $\mathrm{BB}(n)$ grows faster than any computable function and is not computable, a striking instance of simple programs with enormous outputs.
