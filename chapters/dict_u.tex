\chapter*{U}
\label{chap:u}

\term{Unit}
The multiplicative identity 1 in a ring, or an element with a multiplicative inverse.

\term{Unit (Ring Theory)}
An element $u$ of a ring $R$ that has a multiplicative inverse $v$ such that $uv = vu = 1$. The set of all units in a ring forms a group under multiplication, denoted $R^\times$. In $\mathbb{Z}$, the only units are $1$ and $-1$.

\term{Unit Circle}
The circle in the Euclidean plane with radius 1 centered at the origin, defined by $x^2 + y^2 = 1$. It is the locus of points $(\cos \theta, \sin \theta)$ and serves as the basis for the definition of trigonometric functions.

\term{Unit Group}
For a ring $R$, the group $R^\times$ of units, i.e., the elements possessing multiplicative inverses. In the ring of integers of a number field, Dirichlet's unit theorem describes its structure as the product of a finite torsion group and a free abelian group of rank $r_1 + r_2 - 1$. In a field, every nonzero element is a unit, so the unit group is the multiplicative group of the field.

\term{Unit Vector}
A vector whose norm (length) is exactly 1. Any non-zero vector $v$ can be normalized to a unit vector by dividing by its norm: $\hat{v} = v/\|v\|$.

\term{Unitary Matrix}
A complex square matrix $U$ such that $U^* U = U U^* = I$, where $U^*$ is the conjugate transpose. Unitary matrices preserve the inner product and the norm of vectors, meaning they represent isometries of $\mathbb{C}^n$.

\term{Unitary Operator}
A bounded linear operator $U$ on a Hilbert space $H$ such that $\langle Ux, Uy \rangle = \langle x, y \rangle$ for all $x, y \in H$ and $U$ is surjective. They are the infinite-dimensional analogue of unitary matrices.

\term{Universal}
Applying to all cases; a universal quantifier $\forall x$ ranges over every element.

\term{Universal Set}
In a particular context of set theory, the set containing all objects under consideration, denoted by $U$. All other sets in that context are subsets of the universal set.

\term{Universal Mapping Property}
A property of an object that makes it the "best" or "canonical" object of its type, such that any other object of that type factors through it via a unique morphism. Examples include the tensor product and the free group.

\term{Uncountable Set}
An infinite set that cannot be put into one-to-one correspondence with the set of natural numbers $\mathbb{N}$. The set of real numbers $\mathbb{R}$ is uncountable, with cardinality $2^{\aleph_0}$.

\term{Undecidable}
A decision problem for which no algorithm exists that always terminates with the correct answer.

\term{Underdetermined}
A system of equations with fewer equations than unknowns, generally having infinitely many solutions.

\term{Uniform}
A property holding with a single bound or constant independent of position, as in uniform continuity or uniform convergence.

\term{Uniform Continuity}
A property of a function $f: X \to Y$ where for every $\epsilon > 0$, there exists a $\delta > 0$ such that $d(x, y) < \delta \implies d(f(x), f(y)) < \epsilon$ for all $x, y \in X$. Unlike ordinary continuity, $\delta$ depends only on $\epsilon$, not on the point $x$.

\term{Uniform Convergence}
The convergence of a sequence of functions such that the supremum of their errors tends to zero.

\term{Uniform Distribution}
A probability distribution where every value in a given range is equally likely. For a continuous interval $[a, b]$, the probability density function is constant: $f(x) = 1/(b-a)$.

\term{Uniform Space}
A topological space equipped with a uniform structure (a set of entourages) that allows for the definition of uniform continuity and completeness without requiring a metric.

\term{Union}
The set of all elements belonging to at least one of a collection of sets: $A \cup B = \{x : x \in A \text{ or } x \in B\}$.

\term{Unimodular Matrix}
A square matrix with integer entries whose determinant is $\pm 1$. Such matrices are invertible over the integers, meaning their inverse also has integer entries.

\term{Uniqueness Theorem}
A result stating that under certain conditions, a solution to a problem is the only one that exists. For example, the Picard--Lindelöf theorem provides conditions for the uniqueness of solutions to ODEs.

\term{Unipotent Element}
An element $u$ of a ring or group such that $u - 1$ is nilpotent. In matrix theory, a unipotent matrix is one whose only eigenvalue is 1 and is similar to an upper triangular matrix with 1s on the diagonal.

\term{Upper Half-Plane}
The set of complex numbers $\{z \in \mathbb{C} : \operatorname{Im}(z) > 0\}$. It is a canonical domain for the study of modular forms and hyperbolic geometry (the Poincaré half-plane model).

\term{Upper Bound}
A value $M$ such that every element in a set $S$ is less than or equal to $M$. If $M$ is the smallest such value, it is the supremum (least upper bound).

\term{Upper Semi-continuous Function}
A function $f$ is upper semi-continuous at $x$ if for every $\epsilon > 0$, there exists a neighborhood $U$ of $x$ such that $f(y) < f(x) + \epsilon$ for all $y \in U$.

\term{Upper Triangular}
A matrix whose entries below the main diagonal are all zero.

\term{Urysohn Metrization Theorem}
A second-countable regular ($T_3$) space is metrizable. Equivalently, every second-countable normal space embeds in the Hilbert cube. It relies on the Urysohn lemma, which constructs continuous functions separating closed sets.

\term{Use}
The operation of applying a function or rule to arguments in a computation or proof.

\term{U-statistic}
A class of unbiased estimators in statistics that generalize the sample variance. They are constructed as the average of a kernel function applied to all possible subsets of the sample.

\term{Unitary Group}
The group $U(n)$ of all $n \times n$ unitary matrices. It is a compact Lie group and is used to describe symmetries in quantum mechanics.

\term{Unique}
Existing in exactly one instance; a theorem proving uniqueness rules out multiple solutions.

\term{Unique Factorization Domain (UFD)}
An integral domain in which every non-zero non-unit element can be written as a product of prime elements, and this factorization is unique up to reordering and multiplication by units.

\term{Unital Ring}
A ring that possesses a multiplicative identity element $1$ such that $1 \cdot a = a \cdot 1 = a$ for all $a$ in the ring.

\term{Unitary Representation}
A representation of a group $G$ as a group of unitary operators on a Hilbert space. These are the primary objects of study in harmonic analysis and quantum physics.

\term{Unimodular Group}
A locally compact group whose left Haar measure is also a right Haar measure. All compact groups and all abelian groups are unimodular.

\term{Universal Covering Space}
A simply connected space $\tilde{X}$ that covers a space $X$ via a continuous map $p: \tilde{X} \to X$. Every connected, locally path-connected space has a unique universal cover.

\term{Ultrafilter}
A maximal filter on a set $X$. An ultrafilter $U$ has the property that for any subset $A \subseteq X$, either $A \in U$ or $X \setminus A \in U$. They are used in the construction of hyperreal numbers.

\term{Ultraproduct}
A construction in model theory where a family of structures is combined and then quotiented by an ultrafilter. This is used to prove that if a property holds for "most" structures in a family, it holds for the ultraproduct.

\term{Ultrametric Space}
A metric space where the triangle inequality is replaced by the stronger ultrametric inequality: $d(x, z) \leq \max(d(x, y), d(y, z))$. In such spaces, all triangles are isosceles.

\term{Unary Operation}
An operation on a single operand, such as negation $x \mapsto -x$ or complementation.

\term{Unconditional Convergence}
A series $\sum a_n$ converges unconditionally if every rearrangement of its terms converges to the same sum. This is equivalent to absolute convergence in Banach spaces.

\term{Unbounded Operator}
A linear operator between normed spaces that is not bounded (i.e., its operator norm is infinite). Examples include the derivative operator $\frac{d}{dx}$ on $L^2$.

\term{Unit Disc}
The set of complex numbers $z$ such that $|z| \leq 1$. The interior is the open unit disc. Many problems in complex analysis are studied on the unit disc before being mapped to other domains.

\term{Unitary Transformation}
A transformation of a vector space that preserves the inner product. In quantum mechanics, these transformations represent the time-evolution of a closed system.

\term{Roots of Unity}
The complex numbers that satisfy $z^n = 1$ for some positive integer $n$. They are given by $e^{2\pi i k/n}$ for $k = 0, 1, \ldots, n-1$ and form a cyclic group under multiplication.

\term{Univalent Function}
A holomorphic function that is injective (one-to-one). The study of univalent functions is a core part of geometric function theory, notably the Bieberbach conjecture.
