\chapter*{Q}
\label{chap:q}

\term{Q-analog}
A generalization of a mathematical expression where a parameter $q$ is introduced such that the original expression is recovered as $q \to 1$. A classic example is the $q$-binomial coefficient.

\term{Q-matrix}
In the theory of continuous-time Markov chains, the transition rate matrix $Q$ is defined such that the off-diagonal entries $q_{ij}$ are transition rates and the row sums are zero: $q_{ii} = -\sum_{j \neq i} q_{ij}$.

\term{Q-series}
A power series in the variable $q = e^{2\pi i \tau}$, typically used in the theory of modular forms and partitions. The generating function for $p(n)$ is a famous example of a $q$-series.

\term{Quadratic Equation}
An equation of the form $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ where $a \neq 0$. The solutions are given by the quadratic formula $x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$.

\term{Quadratic Form}
A homogeneous polynomial of degree two, expressed as $Q(v) = v^T A v$ for a symmetric matrix $A$. It is used to classify conic sections and determine the nature of critical points via the Hessian matrix.

\term{Quadratic Reciprocity}
A fundamental theorem in number theory stating that for two distinct odd primes $p$ and $q$, the Legendre symbol $\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)\left(\frac{q}{p}\right) = (-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2} \frac{q-1}{2}}$. It relates the solvability of $x^2 \equiv p \pmod{q}$ to $x^2 \equiv q \pmod{p}$.

\term{Quadratic Residue}
An integer $a$ for which the congruence $x^2 \equiv a \pmod{p}$ has a solution.

\term{Quadrature}
The process of calculating the area under a curve, often via numerical approximation. Gaussian quadrature is a highly efficient method that chooses optimal evaluation points (nodes) and weights to integrate polynomials exactly.

\term{Quadrature Domain}
A bounded domain $\Omega \subset \mathbb{C}$ such that the integral of every integrable holomorphic function over $\Omega$ is equal to a finite sum of values of the function and its derivatives at specific points.

\term{Quadrature Rule}
A specific formula used to numerically approximate a definite integral, such as the Trapezoidal rule, Simpson's rule, or the more general Gaussian quadrature weights.

\term{Qualitative}
Describing properties of a solution or system without exact computation, such as stability or long-term behaviour.

\term{Qualitative Analysis}
An approach to mathematics (especially ODEs and dynamical systems) that focuses on the behavior of solutions (e.g., stability, limit cycles) without attempting to find an explicit closed-form solution.

\term{Quantifier}
A logical operator such as $\forall$ (for all) or $\exists$ (there exists) governing the scope of a variable in a formula.

\term{Quantile}
A cut point dividing the range of a probability distribution into contiguous intervals with equal probabilities. The 0.5 quantile is the median; 0.25 and 0.75 quantiles are the first and third quartiles.

\term{Quantile Function}
The inverse of the cumulative distribution function, $Q(p) = \inf\{x : F(x) \geq p\}$.

\term{Quantitative}
Relating to amounts and measurement, expressed numerically.

\term{Quantitative Analysis}
The branch of mathematics and statistics that uses numerical data and mathematical models to identify patterns and make predictions.

\term{Quantity}
A measurable or countable amount; the object of study of arithmetic and algebra.

\term{Quantum State}
A unit vector (or a ray in a Hilbert space) that completely describes the state of a quantum system. The probability of measuring an observable is given by the Born rule $|\langle \psi | \phi \rangle|^2$.

\term{Quarter}
One fourth of a whole, or a quadrant of the plane.

\term{Quarter-turn Symmetry}
A rotational symmetry of $90^\circ$ (or $\pi/2$ radians) about a center point. An object with quarter-turn symmetry is invariant under the group $\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$.

\term{Quasicoherent Sheaf}
An $\mathcal{O}_X$-module on a scheme $X$ that is locally a cokernel of a morphism of free sheaves, equivalently locally associated to a module over an affine ring. The category of quasicoherent sheaves is closed under kernels, cokernels, and extensions, and global sections are computed via Čech cohomology. It includes ideal sheaves and structure sheaves, and contains the coherent sheaves as a subcategory.

\term{Quasiconcave Function}
A function $f$ whose superlevel sets $\{x : f(x) \geq \alpha\}$ are all convex. It is the negative of a quasi-convex function.

\term{Quasiconformal Map}
A homeomorphism between domains in the complex plane that generalizes conformal maps by allowing a bounded amount of "eccentricity." It maps infinitesimal circles to infinitesimal ellipses with bounded aspect ratio.

\term{Quasi-convex Function}
A function $f$ whose sublevel sets $\{x : f(x) \leq \alpha\}$ are all convex sets. Every convex function is quasi-convex, but the converse is not true. They are important in optimization for functions that may not be strictly convex.

\term{Quasigroup}
A set with a binary operation in which division is always possible, so equations $a \ast x = b$ are uniquely solvable.

\term{Quasi-isomorphism}
A morphism between chain complexes that induces an isomorphism on all homology groups. In the derived category, quasi-isomorphisms are inverted to treat complexes as equivalent if they have the same homology.

\term{Quasilinear}
An equation linear in its highest-order derivative terms but nonlinear in the lower terms.

\term{Quasi-metric Space}
A generalization of a metric space where the symmetry axiom $d(x,y) = d(y,x)$ is dropped. These are used in the study of asymmetric distance, such as walking uphill vs. downhill.

\term{Quasi-Newton Method}
An optimization method that approximates the Hessian from gradient differences, such as the BFGS method.

\term{Quasi-periodic Function}
A function that can be approximated by a finite sum of periodic functions with incommensurate frequencies. Unlike periodic functions, they never exactly repeat, but they return arbitrarily close to their initial values.

\term{Quasi-random Sequence}
A sequence of points in a unit cube that is more uniformly distributed than a random sequence. Low-discrepancy sequences (like the Sobol or Halton sequences) are used in Quasi-Monte Carlo integration.

\term{Quaternion Algebra}
The study of algebras over a field that generalize the quaternions. A quaternion algebra is a central simple algebra of dimension 4 over its center.

\term{Quaternion Norm}
The norm of a quaternion $q = a + bi + cj + dk$ is $|q| = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2 + d^2}$. It is a multiplicative norm, meaning $|q_1 q_2| = |q_1| |q_2|$.

\term{Quaternions}
A non-commutative division algebra $\mathbb{H}$ extending complex numbers, with basis $\{1, i, j, k\}$ such that $i^2 = j^2 = k^2 = ijk = -1$. They are used to represent 3D rotations and are fundamental in computer graphics and theoretical physics.

\term{Quine}
A program that outputs a copy of its own source code as its complete output, with no external input. Quines exist for any sufficiently expressive programming language and are related to fixed-point arguments such as the recursion theorem in computability theory; named after W. V. O. Quine, echoing his work on self-reference.

\term{Quintic}
A polynomial or equation of degree 5; the general quintic is not solvable by radicals.

\term{Quintic Equation}
A polynomial equation of degree five. The Abel-Ruffini theorem states that there is no general algebraic solution (using radicals) for all quintic equations, unlike quadratic, cubic, and quartic equations.

\term{Quotient Group}
Given a group $G$ and a normal subgroup $N$, the quotient group $G/N$ is the set of cosets of $N$ in $G$ with the operation $(aN)(bN) = (ab)N$. It represents the structure of $G$ "modulo" $N$.

\term{Quotient Manifold}
A manifold formed by the quotient of a manifold $M$ by a discrete group $G$ acting freely and properly discontinuously. The resulting space $M/G$ inherits a smooth structure.

\term{Quotient Map}
A surjective map $f: X \to Y$ such that a subset $U \subseteq Y$ is open iff $f^{-1}(U)$ is open in $X$. Every quotient map is continuous and open-ish, and it is used to define the quotient topology.

\term{Quotient Ring}
For a ring $R$ and a two-sided ideal $I$, the quotient ring $R/I$ is the set of cosets $\{r + I : r \in R\}$. It allows for the construction of new rings, such as the field $\mathbb{C} \cong \mathbb{R}[x]/(x^2 + 1)$.

\term{Quotient Space}
In topology, the space $X/\sim$ formed by identifying points of $X$ according to an equivalence relation $\sim$. The quotient map $q: X \to X/\sim$ is continuous, and the space inherits the quotient topology.

\term{Quotient Topology}
The finest topology on the quotient of a space by an equivalence relation that makes the quotient map continuous.
