\chapter*{V}
\label{chap:v}

\term{Valuation}
A valuation assigns elements of a field values in an ordered group so that $v(xy)=v(x)+v(y)$ and $v(x+y)\geq\min(v(x),v(y))$. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} valuations measure divisibility and size in number theory and define $p$-adic fields.

\term{Value (Logic)}
A logical value is an assignment, such as true or false, to a formula or its variables. Multi-valued logics allow additional values. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} logical values support formal reasoning, digital circuits, and program verification.

\term{Vandermonde Matrix}
A Vandermonde matrix has entries $V_{ij}=x_j^{i-1}$, so each column contains powers of one number. Its determinant is nonzero exactly when the $x_j$ are distinct. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} it supports polynomial interpolation, coding, and signal reconstruction.

\term{Van Kampen's Theorem}
Van Kampen's theorem computes the fundamental group of a space made from overlapping path-connected pieces using the groups of the pieces and their intersection. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} it calculates loops in spaces assembled from simpler parts.

\term{Variation}
The variation of a function measures its total accumulated change. It has bounded variation on an interval when the supremum of partition sums of absolute changes is finite. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} bounded variation supports integration, signal analysis, and functions with jumps.

\term{Variational Formulation}
A variational formulation rewrites a PDE as an integral equation for a class of test functions, such as $\int\nabla u\cdot\nabla v=\int fv$. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} it provides weak solutions and is the foundation of finite-element methods.

\term{Variational Inequality}
A variational inequality requires a candidate $x^*$ to satisfy $F(x^*)\cdot(x-x^*)\geq0$ for every feasible $x$. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} variational inequalities model constrained optimization, contact mechanics, traffic flow, and games.

\term{Variational Method}
A variational method finds extrema of a functional by studying small changes to a candidate function. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} it derives Euler--Lagrange equations and models mechanics, optics, and PDEs.

\term{Vector}
A vector is an element of a vector space; in $\mathbb R^n$ it is an ordered list of real numbers. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} vectors represent displacement, velocity, forces, data features, and directions.

\term{Vector Field}
A vector field assigns a vector smoothly or regularly to each point of a region or manifold. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} vector fields model velocity, electric and magnetic fields, flows, and gradients.

\term{Vector Space}
A vector space is a set with addition and scalar multiplication satisfying the standard linearity axioms. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} vector spaces provide the language for linear equations, geometry, data analysis, and quantum states.

\term{Vector Bundle}
A vector bundle assigns a vector space to each point of a base space and locally looks like a product $U\times V$. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} vector bundles describe tangent spaces, fields, constraints, and characteristic classes.

\term{Vectorization}
Vectorization converts a matrix into a column vector by stacking its columns. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} it turns matrix equations into linear vector equations and is used in statistics, optimization, and numerical linear algebra.

\term{Velocity}
Velocity is the derivative of position with respect to time, including both speed and direction. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} velocity describes motion, fluid flow, trajectories, and dynamical systems.

\term{Verification}
Verification is the process of checking that a proof, algorithm, or computation satisfies its stated requirements. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} verification supports reliable software, formal mathematics, and safety-critical systems.

\term{Vertex (Graph Theory)}
A vertex is a node of a graph, and edges connect pairs of vertices. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} vertices represent locations, people, states, or data items in network models.

\term{Vertex Cover}
A vertex cover is a set of vertices meeting every edge. A minimum vertex cover has the smallest possible size. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} vertex cover models facility placement, monitoring, and network protection.

\term{Vertex Degree}
The degree of a vertex is the number of incident edges. In a directed graph it is separated into in-degree and out-degree. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} degree measures connectivity, traffic, influence, and network load.

\term{Vertex Connectivity}
Vertex connectivity is the minimum number of vertices whose removal disconnects a graph or leaves one vertex. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} it measures network robustness and resistance to failures.

\term{Vertex (Polytope)}
A vertex of a polytope is a zero-dimensional face and cannot be written as a nontrivial convex combination of other points. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} vertices determine feasible corners in linear programming and polyhedral geometry.

\term{Valued Field}
A valued field is a field together with a valuation that measures size or divisibility. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} valued fields support $p$-adic analysis, number theory, and algebraic geometry.

\term{Variety (Algebraic)}
An algebraic variety is a set defined by polynomial equations, in affine or projective space, together with its geometric structure. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} varieties encode solution sets in algebraic geometry, number theory, and robotics.

\term{Varifold}
A varifold is a measure-theoretic object that generalizes a submanifold and can include singular or nonsmooth surfaces. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} varifolds are used in geometric measure theory and the analysis of minimal surfaces.

\term{Vect}
\textsc{Vect} is the category whose objects are vector spaces over a fixed field and whose morphisms are linear maps. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} it provides a standard setting for linear algebra, tensor products, and representation theory.

\term{Vertical}
Vertical describes a direction perpendicular to the horizontal; a vertical line has undefined slope in Cartesian coordinates. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} vertical and horizontal directions organize geometry, graphs, and physical measurements.

\term{Violation}
A violation is a case in which a rule, inequality, or constraint is not satisfied. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} violations identify errors, infeasible solutions, and failures of physical or statistical assumptions.

\term{Visualisation}
Visualization represents mathematical objects or data with diagrams, plots, or geometric models. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} visualization reveals patterns, structure, uncertainty, and relationships in mathematics and data science.

\term{Voronoi Diagram}
A Voronoi diagram partitions a space into cells according to the nearest seed point. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} it supports nearest-neighbor search, facility planning, geographic analysis, and biology.

\term{Voronoi Cell}
A Voronoi cell is the region of points at least as close to one seed as to every other seed. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} cells define service areas, nearest-site regions, and spatial influence zones.

\term{V-polytope}
A V-polytope is represented as the convex hull of finitely many vertices. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} V-representations describe feasible regions and support conversion between geometric and inequality descriptions.

\term{Variance}
Variance is the expected squared distance from a random variable to its mean, $\operatorname{Var}(X)=\mathbb E[(X-\mu)^2]$. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} variance measures uncertainty and spread in statistics, finance, experiments, and quality control.

\term{V-statistic}
A V-statistic is an average of a function over all ordered tuples of observations, allowing repeated observations. For a kernel $h$ of $m$ variables, it has the form $n^{-m}\sum h(X_{i_1},\ldots,X_{i_m})$, where the sum ranges over all indices. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} V-statistics estimate moments, dependence measures, and goodness-of-fit quantities from data.

\term{V-fold Cross Validation}
V-fold cross-validation divides data into $V$ parts, trains on $V-1$ parts, and tests on the remaining part, repeating the process. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} it estimates predictive performance and helps choose models or hyperparameters.

\term{Vanishing Point}
A vanishing point is the projective image where a family of parallel lines appears to meet in a perspective drawing. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} vanishing points support computer vision, photography, architecture, and 3D reconstruction.

\term{Vanishing Theorem}
A vanishing theorem states that specified cohomology groups are zero under suitable geometric or positivity conditions. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} vanishing theorems simplify classification and existence questions in algebraic and complex geometry.

\term{V-norm}
A V-norm is a context-dependent norm used to measure elements in an operator algebra or sequence space. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} such norms quantify size and convergence in functional analysis.

\term{von Neumann Algebra}
A von Neumann algebra is a *-algebra of bounded operators on a Hilbert space that is closed in the weak operator topology. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} it models observables in quantum theory and supports operator algebras and noncommutative probability.
\term{Shortest Path}
A shortest path minimizes the total edge weight between two graph vertices. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} shortest-path algorithms route networks, plan transport, navigate maps, and optimize logistics.
\term{Universal Property}
A universal property characterizes an object by a unique mapping condition rather than by a particular construction. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} products, free objects, tensor products, and completions are defined canonically this way.

\term{Vitali Set}
A Vitali set is a subset of the real numbers that cannot be assigned a translation-invariant Lebesgue measure. Its construction uses the axiom of choice to select representatives of rational-translation classes. \par\noindent\textbf{Applications:} it demonstrates limits of measure and the role of choice in analysis.
