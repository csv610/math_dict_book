\chapter*{Y}
\label{chap:y}

\term{Yang--Baxter Equation}
The Yang--Baxter equation says that three pairwise interactions give the same final result whichever compatible order is used. \par\noindent Applications: it organizes solvable models, quantum symmetries, statistical mechanics, and knot invariants.

\term{Yang--Mills Theory}
Yang--Mills theory describes physical fields whose forces come from continuous symmetries. It extends electromagnetism to more complicated symmetries and predicts that some particles can behave as if they have mass. \par\noindent Applications: it models the strong and weak nuclear forces and connects particle physics with geometry.

\term{y-axis}
The $y$-axis is the vertical reference line in the coordinate plane. It contains all points whose $x$-coordinate is zero. \par\noindent Applications: it locates points, graphs functions, and measures vertical change.

\term{y-coordinate}
The $y$-coordinate is the second number in an ordered pair $(x,y)$. It tells how far above or below the $x$-axis the point lies. \par\noindent Applications: it describes height, output, position, and vertical change.

\term{Y-Delta Transform}
A Y--Delta transformation replaces a three-branch star circuit by an equivalent triangular circuit, or reverses that replacement. The new circuit has the same behavior at its external terminals. \par\noindent Applications: it simplifies electrical networks, circuit design, power systems, and resistance calculations.

\term{y-intercept}
The $y$-intercept is where a graph crosses the $y$-axis, found by setting $x=0$. \par\noindent Applications: it gives the starting value of a model and helps sketch and interpret graphs.

\term{Yoneda Lemma}
The Yoneda lemma says that an object can be understood completely by studying all the ways it relates to other objects. A compatible collection of such relationships is the same as choosing one element in the object being studied. \par\noindent Applications: it is a central organizing idea in category theory, algebra, geometry, and computer science.

\term{Young Diagram}
A Young diagram is a left-aligned arrangement of boxes whose rows do not get longer as they go down. It gives a picture of how a whole number is partitioned into parts. \par\noindent Applications: Young diagrams organize partitions, permutations, symmetry, and representation theory.

\term{Young's Inequality}
Young's inequality bounds a product by a weighted sum of powers: $ab\leq a^p/p+b^q/q$ when $1/p+1/q=1$. \par\noindent Applications: it controls products in estimates, probability, optimization, differential equations, and signal analysis.

\term{Young Tableau}
A Young tableau fills the boxes of a Young diagram with numbers, usually increasing along rows and columns. \par\noindent Applications: tableaux count arrangements and organize representations, symmetric functions, and combinatorial algorithms.
