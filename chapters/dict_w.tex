\chapter*{W}
\label{chap:w}

\term{Waelbroeck Space}
A topological vector space that is not necessarily locally convex, but where every continuous linear functional is bounded. They appear in the study of generalized functions and distribution theory.

\term{Walk (Graph Theory)}
A sequence of vertices and edges $v_0, e_1, v_1, e_2, v_2, \ldots, e_k, v_k$ such that each edge $e_i$ connects $v_{i-1}$ and $v_i$. Unlike a path, a walk may repeat vertices and edges.

\term{Wallis Product}
The infinite product $\frac{2}{1}\cdot\frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{4}{3}\cdot\frac{4}{5}\cdots = \frac{\pi}{2}$ for $\pi$.

\term{Walsh--Hadamard Transform}
A non- linear transform that decomposes a signal into a sum of Walsh functions. It is the discrete version of the Haar wavelet transform and is used in digital communications and quantum computing.

\term{W-algebra}
A vertex operator algebra associated with a Lie algebra, typically the symmetry algebra of a conformal field theory. They generalize the Virasoro algebra and provide a framework for studying higher-spin symmetries.

\term{Ward Identity}
In quantum field theory, a relation between correlation functions that expresses the symmetry of the underlying action. It is the quantum analogue of Noether's theorem.

\term{Waring's Problem}
The problem of determining whether for every natural number $k$, there exists an integer $g(k)$ such that every natural number is the sum of at most $g(k)$ $k$-th powers. Hilbert proved this in 1909.

\term{Wave Equation}
A second-order partial differential equation $\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} = c^2 \nabla^2 u$ that describes the propagation of waves, such as sound or light, through a medium.

\term{Wavelength}
The spatial period of a wave, the distance between successive identical points such as crests.

\term{Wavelet Transform}
An analysis tool that decomposes a signal into shifted and scaled versions of a "mother wavelet." Unlike the Fourier transform, it provides both time and frequency localization.

\term{Weak Convergence}
A type of convergence in functional analysis: a sequence $x_n$ in a Banach space $X$ converges weakly to $x$ if for every continuous linear functional $f \in X^*$, $f(x_n) \to f(x)$.

\term{Weak-* Convergence}
Convergence of a sequence in the dual of a Banach space, tested against the elements of the original space.

\term{Weak Derivative}
A generalization of the derivative used in the study of Sobolev spaces. A function $g$ is the weak derivative of $f$ if for all test functions $\phi$ with compact support, $\int f \phi' = -\int g \phi$.

\term{Weak Solution}
A solution of a differential equation defined through integration against smooth test functions, allowing functions with fewer derivatives than a classical solution.

\term{Weak Topology}
The coarsest topology on a Banach or locally convex space $X$ for which every continuous linear functional in the dual $X^*$ remains continuous. A sequence converges in the weak topology precisely when it converges weakly. Alaoglu's theorem asserts that the closed unit ball of the dual is compact in the weak-* topology. Weak compactness is closely tied to reflexivity of the space.

\term{Weakly Connected Component}
In a directed graph, a maximal subgraph where any two vertices can be connected by a path regardless of the direction of the edges.

\term{Weighted Graph}
A graph where each edge is assigned a numerical value (weight). These are used to model costs, distances, or capacities in networks.

\term{Weighted Mean}
An average where some data points contribute more than others. The weighted mean is $\frac{\sum w_i x_i}{\sum w_i}$, where $w_i$ are the weights.

\term{Weil Conjecture}
A set of four conjectures (now theorems) concerning the number of points on algebraic varieties over finite fields. They were proven by Dwork, Grothendieck, and Deligne.

\term{Weil Pairing}
A bilinear map $e_n$ that associates two $n$-torsion points on an elliptic curve with an $n$-th root of unity. It is fundamental in the study of the group structure of elliptic curves and in cryptography.

\term{Weil Transform}
A specific integral transform used in the theory of automorphic forms and representation theory of $GL_n$.

\term{Weierstrass Function}
A function that is continuous everywhere but differentiable nowhere. It was the first published example of such a function, challenging the contemporary intuition that continuous functions must be almost everywhere differentiable.

\term{Weierstrass Product Theorem}
A result in complex analysis stating that every entire function can be represented as a product involving its zeros and an exponential of an entire function.

\term{Weierstrass Theorem (Approximation)}
A theorem stating that every continuous function on a closed interval can be uniformly approximated by a polynomial. This is the foundation for the Stone--Weierstrass theorem.

\term{Weight (Representation Theory)}
An eigenvalue of the action of a Cartan subalgebra on a representation of a semisimple Lie algebra. The set of all weights forms a weight system that characterizes the representation.

\term{Weight (Graph Theory)}
The value assigned to an edge in a weighted graph. In the context of spanning trees, the weight of the tree is the sum of its edges' weights.

\term{Well-Defined}
A construction or definition that is independent of the choice of representative, as required for functions on quotient sets.

\term{Well-founded Relation}
A binary relation on a set such that every non-empty subset has a minimal element. This property is necessary for the validity of transfinite induction.

\term{Well-Ordered}
A total order in which every non-empty subset has a least element; the natural numbers are well-ordered.

\term{Well-ordering Principle}
The axiom that every non-empty set of positive integers contains a least element. It is equivalent to the axiom of choice for arbitrary sets.

\term{Well-Posed Problem}
A problem is well-posed in the sense of Hadamard if it satisfies three conditions: a solution exists, the solution is unique, and the solution depends continuously on the data (stability). If any of these fail, the problem is said to be ill-posed.

\term{Weyl Group}
A finite group associated with a root system, generated by reflections in the hyperplanes orthogonal to the roots. It describes the symmetries of the root system.

\term{Weyl Algebra}
The algebra of differential operators with polynomial coefficients. It is the simplest example of a non-commutative Noetherian ring and is used in the study of D-modules.

\term{Weyl Character Formula}
A formula for the character of an irreducible representation of a semisimple Lie algebra, expressed as a ratio of two sums over the Weyl group.

\term{Weyl Tensor}
The part of the Riemann curvature tensor that remains after removing the Ricci curvature. It describes the "tidal force" or the distortion of a shape by gravity in general relativity.

\term{Weyl's Law}
An asymptotic formula for the distribution of eigenvalues of the Laplacian on a bounded domain: $N(\lambda) \sim \frac{\omega_n \operatorname{vol}(\Omega)}{(2\pi)^n} \lambda^{n/2}$.

\term{Weyl's Inequality}
A set of bounds on the eigenvalues of a sum of two Hermitian matrices $A$ and $B$ in terms of the eigenvalues of $A$ and $B$ individually.

\term{Whitney Embedding Theorem}
Every smooth manifold of dimension $n$ embeds smoothly into $\mathbb{R}^{2n}$ and immerses into $\mathbb{R}^{2n-1}$. The bound is sharp: the real projective plane, for instance, requires $\mathbb{R}^4$. It is closely related to the Nash embedding theorems for Riemannian manifolds.

\term{Wigner D-matrix}
Matrices that provide the representation of the rotation group $SO(3)$ on the space of spherical harmonics. They are used extensively in quantum mechanics to describe angular momentum.

\term{Wigner's Semicircle Law}
A result stating that the eigenvalues of a large random symmetric matrix follow a semicircular distribution. This is a central result in random matrix theory.

\term{Winding Number}
An integer that represents the total number of times a closed curve travels counter-clockwise around a point. It is defined as the index of the curve in complex analysis.

\term{Witt Vector}
A sequence of elements from a ring used to construct a ring of characteristic $0$ (the Witt ring) from a ring of characteristic $p$. They are essential in the study of p-adic cohomology.

\term{Witt Group}
A group that classifies quadratic forms over a field. Two forms are equivalent in the Witt group if they differ by a sum of hyperbolic planes.

\term{Word}
A finite sequence of symbols from an alphabet, the basic object of formal language theory.

\term{Wronskian}
A determinant of a matrix formed by a function and its successive derivatives. If the Wronskian of a set of functions is non-zero at a point, the functions are linearly independent.

\term{Well-Ordering Theorem}
The theorem that every set can be well-ordered, i.e., equipped with a total order in which every non-empty subset has a least element. Equivalent to the axiom of choice and Zorn's lemma. The natural numbers with their usual order are well-ordered, but the real numbers are not.
