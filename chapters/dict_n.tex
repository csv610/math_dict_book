\chapter*{N}
\label{chap:n}

\term{Nash Equilibrium}
A strategy profile in a game where no player can increase their payoff by unilaterally changing their strategy, given that other players keep their strategies unchanged.

\term{Natural Transformation}
A structure-preserving map between functors, assigning to each object a morphism commuting with the functorial maps.

\term{Navier--Stokes Equations}
A set of partial differential equations that describe the motion of viscous fluid substances. Solving them for the case of 3D incompressible flow is one of the Millennium Prize Problems.

\term{Necessary and Sufficient Condition}
A condition $A$ that is both necessary and sufficient for $B$, meaning $A$ is true if and only if $B$ is true ($A \iff B$).

\term{Necessary Condition}
A condition that must be true for an event to occur, but whose truth does not guarantee that the event will occur. $A$ is necessary for $B$ if $B \implies A$.

\term{Neighborhood (Topology)}
A neighborhood of a point $x$ in a topological space is a set $V$ that contains an open set $U$ such that $x \in U \subseteq V$.

\term{Nested Set}
A family of sets each of which is contained in the next, $S_1 \subseteq S_2 \subseteq \cdots$.

\term{Net (Topology)}
A generalization of a sequence, where the index set is a directed set rather than the natural numbers. Nets are used to characterize compactness in arbitrary topological spaces.

\term{Neumann Boundary Condition}
A boundary condition that specifies the value of the normal derivative of a function on the boundary of a domain: $\frac{\partial u}{\partial n} = g$.

\term{Newcomb's Paradox}
A puzzle about an infallible predictor who has placed money in boxes: one box always contains \$1000 and a second contains \$1,000,000 exactly when the predictor expects you to take only the second box. Two-boxing dominates in payoff, but the predictor's accuracy makes one-boxing optimal, exposing a tension between causal and evidential decision theory.

\term{Newton Polytopes}
The convex hull of the exponents of the monomials in a polynomial, used to study the behavior of the polynomial's zeros and its Newton-Puiseux series.

\term{Newton's Method}
A root-finding algorithm that uses the first two terms of the Taylor series to approximate the root of a real-valued function: $x_{n+1} = x_n - \frac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)}$.

\term{Nilpotent Element}
An element $x$ of a ring such that $x^n = 0$ for some positive integer $n$. The smallest such $n$ is the index of nilpotency.

\term{Nilpotent Ideal}
An ideal $I$ such that $I^n = 0$ for some $n \geq 1$. Every element of a nilpotent ideal is a nilpotent element.

\term{Nilpotent Ring}
A ring where every element is nilpotent, or equivalently, the ring itself is a nilpotent ideal of itself.

\term{Nodal Domain}
A connected component of the set where an eigenfunction of the Laplacian (such as a vibration mode of a drum) is non-zero.

\term{Node (Graph Theory)}
A fundamental unit of a graph, also called a vertex. A graph consists of a set of nodes and a set of edges connecting them.

\term{Noetherian Module}
A module $M$ such that every ascending chain of submodules stabilizes. For a ring $R$, $R$ is Noetherian iff every finitely generated $R$-module is Noetherian.

\term{Noetherian Ring}
A ring $R$ that satisfies the ascending chain condition on its ideals: every chain of ideals $I_1 \subseteq I_2 \subseteq \cdots$ eventually stabilizes.

\term{Non-archimedean Field}
A field with a valuation $|\cdot|$ that satisfies the strong triangle inequality: $|x+y| \leq \max(|x|, |y|)$. The $p$-adic numbers $\mathbb{Q}_p$ are the primary example.

\term{Non-commutative Geometry}
A generalization of geometry where the algebra of "functions" on a space is not required to be commutative, treating the algebra itself as the primary geometric object.

\term{Nonconvex Optimization}
An optimization problem whose feasible set or objective is not convex, so it may have multiple local optima.

\term{Non-degenerate}
A form or matrix for which the only solution to $B(x, y) = 0$ for all $y$ is $x = 0$; a matrix with full rank.

\term{Non-Euclidean Geometry}
Geometries where the parallel postulate of Euclid is replaced. Examples include hyperbolic geometry (where multiple parallel lines exist) and elliptic geometry (where no parallel lines exist).

\term{Nonlinear}
A system or equation not governed by linearity, so superposition does not apply.

\term{Non-orientable Surface}
A surface that does not have a consistent "side," such as the Möbius strip or the Klein bottle. A surface is non-orientable iff it contains a Möbius strip.

\term{Non-standard Analysis}
A branch of mathematics that uses infinitesimals and infinite numbers to provide a rigorous foundation for the intuitive "infinitesimal" calculus of Leibniz and Newton.

\term{Normal Bundle}
A vector bundle over a submanifold $M$ whose fibers are the normal spaces to $M$ at each point.

\term{Normal Distribution}
Also known as the Gaussian distribution. A continuous probability distribution characterized by its bell-shaped curve, defined by its mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$.

\term{Normal Form}
A canonical representation of a mathematical object, such as the Jordan Normal Form for matrices or the Smith Normal Form for matrices over a PID.

\term{Normality}
The property of a topological space in which disjoint closed sets have disjoint open neighbourhoods.

\term{Normalization}
The process of scaling a vector or function so that its norm is equal to 1. In probability, it ensures that the total probability of a distribution is 1.

\term{Normalizer (Group Theory)}
For a subgroup $H \subseteq G$, the normalizer $N_G(H)$ is the largest subgroup of $G$ in which $H$ is normal: $N_G(H) = \{g \in G : gHg^{-1} = H\}$.

\term{Normal Map}
In homotopy theory, a map $f: X \to Y$ that is a "normal" map in the sense of surgery theory, typically involving a bundle map from the normal bundle of $X$ to a bundle over $Y$.

\term{Normal Operator}
An operator that commutes with its adjoint, $TT^* = T^*T$; such operators are unitarily diagonalizable.

\term{Normal Space}
The orthogonal complement of the tangent space at a point $p$ on a manifold $M \subset \mathbb{R}^n$.

\term{Normal Subgroup}
A subgroup $N$ of $G$ that is invariant under conjugation: $gNg^{-1} = N$ for all $g \in G$. Normal subgroups are exactly the kernels of group homomorphisms.

\term{Normed Space}
A vector space equipped with a norm. A normed space is a metric space with $d(x,y) = \|x-y\|$. If it is complete, it is called a Banach space.

\term{Norm (Vector Space)}
A function $\|\cdot\|: V \to [0, \infty)$ that satisfies: (1) $\|v\|=0 \iff v=0$, (2) $\|\alpha v\| = |\alpha| \|v\|$, and (3) $\|u+v\| \leq \|u\|+\|v\|$ (the triangle inequality).

\term{Null Hypothesis}
In statistics, the hypothesis $H_0$ that there is no effect or no difference between groups. It is the hypothesis that the researcher tries to reject or fail to reject.

\term{Nullity}
The dimension of the null space of a linear map; by the rank-nullity theorem, $\operatorname{rank} + \operatorname{nullity} = \dim V$.

\term{Null Set}
A set of measure zero; the empty set is sometimes called a null set.

\term{Null Space}
The set of all vectors $v$ such that $Av = 0$ for a matrix $A$. Also called the kernel of the linear transformation.

\term{Nullstellensatz}
See Hilbert's Nullstellensatz: the correspondence between ideals of a polynomial ring and algebraic varieties.

\term{Null Vector}
A vector of zero norm; in relativity, a lightlike vector with $g(v, v) = 0$.

\term{Number Theory}
The study of the properties of integers $\mathbb{Z}$. It is divided into elementary number theory, analytic number theory, and algebraic number theory.

\term{Numerical Analysis}
The study of algorithms that use numerical approximation for the problems of mathematical analysis, focusing on convergence, stability, and error bounds.

\term{Numerical Optimization}
The design and analysis of algorithms for minimizing or maximizing functions numerically.

\term{Numerical Quadrature}
The numerical approximation of definite integrals, such as by the trapezoidal rule or Gaussian quadrature.

\term{Numerical Stability}
The property of an algorithm that small perturbations of the input cause only small perturbations of the output.

\term{Numeric Integration}
The approximation of definite integrals by discrete sums, such as the trapezoidal rule or Simpson's rule.

\term{Nyquist Frequency}
In signal processing, the minimum rate at which a signal must be sampled to avoid aliasing, equal to half the maximum frequency component of the signal.
