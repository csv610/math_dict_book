\chapter*{T}
\label{chap:t}

\term{T-distribution}
A probability distribution used in statistics when the sample size is small and the population variance is unknown. As the degrees of freedom increase, it converges to the standard normal distribution.

\term{T-test}
A statistical hypothesis test used to determine if there is a significant difference between the means of two groups. It is the most common test for comparing small sample sets.

\term{Tableau (Combinatorics)}
A filling of a Young diagram with positive integers. Standard Young Tableaux are used in the representation theory of the symmetric group and in the study of partitions.

\term{T-norm (Triangular Norm)}
A binary operation used in fuzzy logic to generalize the intersection of sets. It must be commutative, associative, monotonic, and satisfy $T(a, 1) = a$.

\term{T-conorm}
The dual operation to the T-norm in fuzzy logic, generalizing the union of sets. It is defined as $S(a, b) = 1 - T(1-a, 1-b)$.

\term{Torsion (Group Theory)}
An element $g$ of a group is a torsion element if it has finite order. A group is a torsion group if all its elements are torsion elements.

\term{Torsion}
See \term{Torsion Tensor}.

\term{Torsion-free Group}
A group in which the only element of finite order is the identity. For example, the additive group of integers $\mathbb{Z}$ is torsion-free.

\term{Torsion Point}
A point $P$ on an elliptic curve such that $nP = \mathcal{O}$ for some positive integer $n$. The set of torsion points of order $n$ forms a group isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$.

\term{Torsion Tensor}
A tensor field on a manifold with an affine connection $\nabla$ defined by $T(X, Y) = \nabla_X Y - \nabla_Y X - [X, Y]$. The Levi--Civita connection is the unique torsion-free connection.

\term{Torsion Subgroup}
The subgroup of an abelian group consisting of all its torsion elements. It is the direct sum of the $p$-primary components of the group.

\term{Tacnode}
A singular point of an algebraic curve where two branches share a common tangent line. It is a type of degenerate singularity.

\term{Tangent}
A line touching a curve at a single point with the same direction as the curve, or the trigonometric function $\tan x = \sin x / \cos x$.

\term{Tangent Space}
A vector space $T_p M$ associated with each point $p$ of a smooth manifold $M$, consisting of all tangent vectors to curves passing through $p$.

\term{Tangent Bundle}
The disjoint union of all tangent spaces of a manifold $M$. It is a smooth manifold of dimension $2n$ where $n$ is the dimension of $M$.

\term{Tangent Line}
The best linear approximation to a curve at a point, whose slope is the derivative.

\term{Tangent Vector}
An object that represents the direction and rate of change of a curve at a given point. Formally, it is a derivation on the algebra of smooth functions.

\term{Tautological Bundle}
The line bundle over a Grassmannian $\operatorname{Gr}(k, V)$ where the fiber over a point (a subspace $W$) is the subspace $W$ itself.

\term{Tautology (Logic)}
A formula in propositional logic that is true under all possible interpretations of its variables.

\term{Taylor Series}
An infinite sum of terms expressed in terms of the function's derivatives at a single point: $f(x) = \sum \frac{f^{(n)}(a)}{n!} (x-a)^n$.

\term{Taylor's Theorem}
A result that provides a way to approximate a differentiable function near a point using a polynomial, with a quantified remainder term.

\term{Teichmüller Space}
The space of complex structures on a fixed topological surface. It is used to study the moduli space of Riemann surfaces.

\term{Teichmüller Theory}
The study of the mapping class group and the geometry of Teichmüller space, relating hyperbolic geometry and complex analysis.

\term{T-matrix (Scattering Matrix)}
A matrix used in quantum mechanics and nuclear physics to describe the relationship between the initial state (plane wave) and the final state (scattered wave).

\term{Temperate Distribution}
A distribution (generalized function) that grows at most polynomially at infinity. They are the dual of the Schwarz space and are the natural objects for the Fourier transform.

\term{Tempered Distribution}
A continuous linear functional on the Schwartz space of rapidly decreasing smooth functions. The space of tempered distributions is the correct setting for the Fourier transform of distributions, since the Fourier transform is an automorphism of the Schwartz space. Examples include the delta function, the principal value of $1/x$, and derivatives of polynomials. It relates to Sobolev spaces of negative order, which consist of tempered distributions.

\term{Tensor}
A multilinear map from a product of vector spaces to the field of scalars. Tensors generalize scalars, vectors, and matrices to any rank.

\term{Tensor Product}
A construction that creates a new vector space from two others, $V \otimes W$, characterized by the property that bilinear maps from $V \times W$ are in one-to-one correspondence with linear maps from $V \otimes W$.

\term{Tensor Algebra}
The graded algebra formed by the direct sum of all tensor powers of a vector space: $T(V) = \bigoplus_{k=0}^\infty V^{\otimes k}$.

\term{Tensor Field}
An assignment of a tensor to each point of a manifold, varying smoothly as a function of the point.

\term{Tensor Analysis}
The study of tensors and their transformations, fundamental to general relativity and differential geometry.

\term{Term (Algebra)}
A single monomial in a polynomial, consisting of a coefficient and variables raised to non-negative integer powers.

\term{Tessellation}
A tiling of the plane or higher-dimensional space by polygons or cells that meet edge-to-edge, with no overlaps and no gaps. Regular and semi-regular tilings classify the monohedral tilings by regular polygons, and the seventeen wallpaper groups describe the periodic tilings with symmetry. Voronoi and Delaunay tessellations partition space from a discrete point set. Tessellations are central in geometry, discrete mathematics, and the classification of crystallographic patterns.

\term{Ternary Operation}
A function that takes three arguments and produces a single output. An example is the ternary operator in programming languages.

\term{Ternary Ring}
A generalization of a ring where the multiplication is a ternary operation satisfying specific axioms, often used in the study of non-associative algebras.

\term{Theorem}
A mathematical statement that has been rigorously proved from axioms and previously established results.

\term{Theory (Logic)}
A set of sentences in a formal language that is closed under logical consequence.

\term{Theta Function}
A special holomorphic function on the upper half-plane that is quasi-periodic. They are used in the study of abelian varieties and modular forms.

\term{Theta Series}
A series whose coefficients are the number of ways to represent an integer as a sum of squares. They are examples of modular forms.

\term{Theorema Egregium}
Gauss's "Remarkable Theorem," which states that the Gaussian curvature of a surface is an intrinsic invariant, meaning it depends only on the first fundamental form.

\term{Third Isomorphism Theorem}
A theorem in group theory: if $N$ and $M$ are normal subgroups of a group $G$ with $N \subseteq M$, then $(G/N)/(M/N) \cong G/M$. The same statement holds for rings and modules, with the appropriate quotients.

\term{Third Root}
The cube root of a number; for any real $x$, there is exactly one real $y$ such that $y^3 = x$.

\term{Thomson's Lamp}
A supertask in which a lamp is turned on at $t=1/2$, off at $t=3/4$, on at $t=7/8$, and so on, completing infinitely many toggles before $t=1$. At $t=1$ the lamp is neither on nor off in any consistent way, showing that the limit of a process need not be determined by the process itself.

\term{Three-dimensional}
Having three degrees of freedom, described by three coordinates as in space $\mathbb{R}^3$.

\term{Tilde (Operator)}
A symbol used in mathematics to denote a modified version of an object or a lapped operation (e.g., $\tilde{f}$ for a transformed function).

\term{Time Complexity}
A measure of the amount of time an algorithm takes to run as a function of the length of the input, typically expressed using Big O notation.

\term{Tikhonov Regularization}
A method of regularization for ill-posed problems, which adds a penalty term (usually the $L_2$ norm) to the objective function to stabilize the solution.

\term{Tiling}
The covering of a plane or space with one or more shapes (tiles) such that there are no gaps or overlaps.

\term{Tiling Theory}
The study of the properties of tilings, including whether a set of tiles can cover the plane (the tiling problem).

\term{Time-reversal Symmetry}
A symmetry in physics where the laws of motion remain invariant if the direction of time is reversed ($t \to -t$).

\term{Timelike Curve}
A curve in Minkowski spacetime whose tangent vector is always timelike, meaning it represents the worldline of a massive particle.

\term{Timelike Vector}
A vector $v$ in Lorentzian space such that its norm is negative (using signature $(-,+,+,+)$).

\term{Tits Building}
A simplicial complex that provides a geometric way to study the structure of groups of Lie type.

\term{Tits Alternative}
A theorem stating that every finitely generated subgroup of a linear group is either virtually solvable or contains a free group of rank 2.

\term{Tits Group}
A specific finite simple group related to the Lie algebra of type $E_k$.

\term{Toeplitz Matrix}
A matrix where each descending diagonal from left to right is constant. These arise in signal processing and the study of linear operators.

\term{Toeplitz Operator}
An operator on a Hardy space defined by multiplication by a function $f \in L^\infty(\mathbb{T})$ followed by projection onto the Hardy space.

\term{Topological Invariant}
A property of a topological space that is preserved under homeomorphisms, such as compactness, connectedness, and the fundamental group.

\term{Topological Space}
A set equipped with a collection of open sets satisfying the axioms of union, finite intersection, and containing the empty set and the whole set.

\term{Topology}
The branch of mathematics studying properties preserved under continuous deformations, formalised through open sets.

\term{Topos}
A category with finite limits, exponentials, and a subobject classifier, generalizing the category of sets. Elementary topoi model constructive mathematics through their internal intuitionistic logic, interpreted via the subobject classifier. Grothendieck topoi are the categories of sheaves on a site, providing a unified setting for cohomology. Topos theory underpins forcing and independence proofs in set theory and forms a foundation for homotopy type theory.

\term{Total Derivative}
The derivative of a function that depends on multiple variables, where those variables are themselves functions of a single independent variable.

\term{Total Differential}
The linear part $df = \sum \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_i} dx_i$ of the change of a differentiable function of several variables.

\term{Total Order}
A partial order in which every pair of elements is comparable. Examples include the standard order on the real numbers.

\term{Total Variation}
A measure of the total amount of oscillation of a function. A function has bounded variation if the sum of the absolute differences of its values over any partition is finite.

\term{Total Variation Diminishing (TVD)}
A property of numerical schemes for solving hyperbolic PDEs that prevents the creation of spurious oscillations near discontinuities.

\term{Touchard Polynomials}
A sequence of polynomials that count the number of partitions of a set of $n$ elements into $k$ blocks.

\term{Tournament (Graph Theory)}
A directed graph formed by orienting every edge of a complete undirected graph.

\term{Torsion-free Abelian Group}
An abelian group where the only element of finite order is the identity. Every such group is a subgroup of a vector space over $\mathbb{Q}$.

\term{Trace}
The sum of the diagonal entries of a square matrix, $\operatorname{tr}(A) = \sum_i A_{ii}$, invariant under change of basis.

\term{Transcendence Degree}
The largest number of elements of a field extension $L/K$ that are algebraically independent over $K$; equivalently, the cardinality of a transcendence basis, which is well-defined because all such bases have the same size. It measures the transcendental dimension of the extension. In algebraic geometry it is the dimension of an algebraic variety. It is central to proofs of transcendence, including the Lindemann--Weierstrass theorem and Hilbert's seventh problem.

\term{Transcendental Extension}
A field extension containing at least one element that is not algebraic over the base field.

\term{Transfinite Induction}
A generalization of mathematical induction to sets that are not countable, using well-ordered sets and ordinal numbers.

\term{Transfinite Number}
A number representing the cardinality of an infinite set, such as $\aleph_0$ (the cardinality of $\mathbb{N}$) or $\mathfrak{c}$ (the cardinality of $\mathbb{R}$).

\term{Transfinite Recursion}
A method of defining functions on ordinals by specifying the value at each step based on the values at all previous steps.

\term{Transformation}
A function mapping a space to itself or to another space, such as a translation, rotation, or linear map.

\term{Transformation (Linear)}
A map between vector spaces that preserves addition and scalar multiplication.

\term{Transformation (Möbius)}
A map of the form $f(z) = \frac{az+b}{cz+d}$ where $ad-bc \neq 0$. These are the automorphisms of the Riemann sphere.

\term{Transitive Action}
A group action where for any two points $x, y \in X$, there exists a group element $g \in G$ such that $g \cdot x = y$.

\term{Translation}
A transformation that moves every point in a space by the same distance in the same direction.

\term{Transpose}
The matrix $A^T$ obtained by interchanging rows and columns of $A$, with $(A^T)_{ij} = A_{ji}$.

\term{Transversality}
A property of two submanifolds intersecting such that their tangent spaces together span the tangent space of the ambient manifold.

\term{Transverse Intersection}
An intersection of two manifolds where the transversality condition is met. Transverse intersections are stable under small perturbations.

\term{Transposition (Permutation)}
A permutation that swaps two elements and leaves all others fixed. Every permutation can be written as a product of transpositions.

\term{Transport (Parallel)}
The process of moving a vector along a curve on a manifold such that its covariant derivative along the curve is zero.

\term{Trapezium}
A quadrilateral with no parallel sides in British usage, or with at least one pair of parallel sides in American usage.

\term{Trapezoidal Rule}
A numerical integration method that approximates the area under a curve by dividing it into trapezoids.

\term{Tree}
A connected acyclic graph; a tree on $n$ vertices has exactly $n-1$ edges.

\term{Triangle Inequality}
A fundamental property of metrics: $d(x,z) \leq d(x,y) + d(y,z)$. Geometrically, the shortest distance between two points is a straight line.

\term{Triangulated Category}
An additive category equipped with a shift functor and a class of distinguished triangles satisfying the axioms TR1--TR4, generalizing the homotopy category of complexes. Distinguished triangles give rise to mapping cones and the octahedral axiom, which encodes their compatibility. Derived categories arise as localizations of homotopy categories of complexes with respect to quasi-isomorphisms, following Verdier's theory of localization of triangulated categories.

\term{Trivial Group}
The group consisting of only the identity element. It is the unique group of order 1.

\term{Trivial Ring}
The ring containing only one element ($0=1$), often excluded from the definition of rings.

\term{Trivial Topology}
The topology on a set where the only open sets are the empty set and the set itself.

\term{Truncation Error}
The error in a numerical scheme caused by replacing the exact equation with a discrete approximation.

\term{Turán's Theorem}
An $n$-vertex graph containing no $K_{r+1}$ subgraph has at most $(1 - 1/r)n^2/2$ edges, with equality attained by the complete $r$-partite Turán graph. It is regarded as the founding result of extremal graph theory.

\term{Turing Machine}
The canonical mathematical model of computation, consisting of a tape, a read--write head, a finite control, and a transition function. The Church--Turing thesis states that every effectively computable function is computable by a Turing machine. The halting problem is undecidable for this model. Universal Turing machines can simulate any other machine, and resource bounds on them define time and space complexity classes.

\term{Twin Primes}
Pairs of primes differing by 2, such as $(3,5)$ and $(11,13)$; the twin prime conjecture asserts infinitely many exist.

\term{Two Envelopes Paradox}
You are offered two envelopes, one with twice the money of the other; a naive expectation argument suggests you should always switch, since the other envelope appears to hold $1.25$ times your envelope on average. The argument is fallacious because it conflates conditional expectation with a fixed distribution; the paradox illuminates problems in defining a uniform distribution over unbounded values.

\term{Two's Complement}
The binary representation of signed integers used in computers, where negation is performed by inverting bits and adding one.

\term{Tychonoff's Theorem}
The product of compact topological spaces is compact in the product topology. It is equivalent to the axiom of choice, and it underlies the Banach--Alaoglu theorem in functional analysis.

\term{Type Theory}
A formal system that assigns types to terms to constrain and organize computation and proofs. The simply typed lambda calculus is the basic such system, and dependent type theory extends it with types depending on terms, as in Martin--Löf type theory. The Curry--Howard correspondence identifies types with propositions and programs with proofs. Homotopy type theory and modern proof assistants are built on these foundations.
\term{Stirling Number}
Stirling numbers of the first kind $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}n\\k\end{smallmatrix}\right]$ count permutations of $n$ elements with $k$ cycles; those of the second kind $\left\{\begin{smallmatrix}n\\k\end{smallmatrix}\right\}$ count partitions of an $n$-element set into $k$ non-empty blocks. They satisfy recurrence relations and appear in the expansion of falling and rising factorials.
\term{Strong Duality}
The property that the optimal values of the primal and dual optimisation problems are equal. In convex programming, strong duality holds under mild constraint qualifications such as Slater's condition. When it fails, a nonzero duality gap remains. Strong duality is the theoretical basis for many algorithms in convex optimisation and machine learning.
